Learn Dashboard Design with Phoebe

A dashboard is a screen that answers a question someone has on a schedule. Everything on it earns its place by the decision it changes and the glance it survives. Six sessions on getting that right: who looks and what they decide, the first glance and visual weight, density and grouping, colour as the state of a card, interaction and a five-user test, and a bench where you set red, amber and green on 581 real days of orders and deliveries and watch the false alarms and the missed changes get counted in your browser.

The six sessions 🧭 · from the person who looks to the colour they trust

The running case is Saltmarsh, a constructed home-goods retailer. Priya, the managing director, has asked for "a dashboard". Tomas, the analyst, is designing the weekly operations screen that Nia, the operations lead, opens at the Monday 09:00 stand-up: are deliveries slipping, did demand fall, did the money fall, where is the backlog. Saltmarsh and everyone in it are invented for teaching, and every page says so. The numbers on its cards are real: daily aggregates from the Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist, named as the source wherever they appear.

👀 Session 1 · foundations

Who looks, and what they decide

User, question, decision, glance. Few's definition read at source, a report against a dashboard against an analysis, and the four-line brief that decides what goes on the screen before anything is drawn.

▶ Start here 🔍 Session 2 · core

Hierarchy and the first glance

Visual weight as size times contrast times position, the reading order, the one number, the squint test. What NN/g's F-pattern really measured and what Miller's seven does not say about tiles.

the one number ▦ Session 3 · core

Density, grouping and white space

One screen. Tiles against tables, grouping by the question and not by the data source, and progressive disclosure: what is on the screen, what is one click away, what is a report.

group by question 🟩 Session 4 · deeper

Colour as state

The KPI card: value, delta, context band. Red, amber and green as the state of a card and never as decoration, colour never the only channel, colour-blind safety, and when colour should mean nothing.

the KPI card 🧪 Session 5 · deeper

Interaction, defaults and the usability test

Filters, drill and the default view most people will ever see. One five-user task test, read at source with its caveats, and what "gets used" actually measures.

five users, three tasks 🚦 Session 6 · bench night

The red-amber-green bench

581 real days. Set the thresholds on a late-deliveries card and the bench counts false-alarm days, missed changes and time in each colour. Launch thresholds, the series' spread, a context band, persistence, and two buttons that make it worse.
